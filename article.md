# Green, and broken

Every gate was green. Fifty-two unit tests, five end-to-end runs, a live-model evaluation passing all nine of its assertions across three personas and three consecutive runs, a container that built and booted and served both faces of the app. By the build brief's own definition of done, the tool was finished. Then someone opened it and started an interview — and the coverage rail, the single element on screen that tells you the interview is actually working, sat at 0 of 12 while the assistant held a perfectly pleasant conversation and recorded nothing at all.

None of those gates were cosmetic. Lint, typecheck, and build are table stakes, but past them sat fifty-two unit and integration tests aimed at the parts that carry real risk: every illegal tool call rejected, every coverage transition checked against a state machine, every generated specification run through a schema validator. Five browser tests drove the whole flow end to end — an architect creating a campaign, issuing a link, an interview completing, a specification downloading. And then the one I trusted most: a second language model, told to play a process expert, ran an entire interview while nine assertions graded the outcome — full coverage, an honest "I don't know" recorded rather than guessed, the numeric approval thresholds captured, the specification valid and free of any email address. It passed for three different personas, three times each, back to back. That is a stronger bar than most software clears before it ships. And all it proved was that the code did what I had told it to do.
